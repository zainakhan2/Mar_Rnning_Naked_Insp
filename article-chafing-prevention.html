The Runner's Guide to Preventing Chafing (Yes, Everywhere)

Nobody talks about chafing until it happens to them. Then it's all they can talk about — usually while wincing in a post-run shower. Here's how to prevent it, where it strikes, and what to do when prevention fails.

Where Chafing Happens (And Why)

Chafing occurs wherever skin rubs against skin, or skin rubs against fabric, repeatedly for an extended period. The friction generates heat. The heat creates irritation. Add sweat (which contains salt that acts as an abrasive) and you have a recipe for raw, angry skin.

The Hot Zones

  • Inner thighs — the #1 chafing zone for runners, especially in shorts without a liner
  • Sports bra band — the elastic band under your breasts rubs with every breath. By mile 10: fire
  • Nipples — yes, women too. Bra seams + sweat + distance = problem
  • Underarms — arm swing creates repetitive friction in the armpit area
  • Waistband — shorts, leggings, or running belt waistband rubbing against hip bones
  • Between toes — wet socks + friction = blisters (chafing's cousin)

Prevention: The Three Layers

Layer 1: Lubrication

Body Glide ($8-$12) is the gold standard. Apply to every hot zone before your run. It creates a invisible barrier that reduces friction without feeling greasy. Reapply on runs over 90 minutes — carry a small stick in your running belt.

Alternatives: Squirrel's Nut Butter (natural ingredients, works great but smells like peanut butter), Vaseline (effective but greasy — stains clothes), and deodorant (in a pinch, solid deodorant applied to inner thighs works).

Layer 2: Clothing

Moisture-wicking fabrics only. Cotton absorbs sweat and holds it against your skin — the worst possible fabric for preventing chafing. Nylon, polyester, or merino wool wick moisture away from skin.

Seam-free or flat-seam construction. Raised seams are chafing factories. Look for running clothes with flatlock seams or seamless construction, especially in bras and underwear.

Compression shorts or liners. Running shorts with a built-in brief liner prevent inner-thigh chafing entirely by eliminating skin-on-skin contact. If your shorts don't have a liner: wear compression shorts underneath.

Layer 3: Fit

Too tight = fabric pressing into skin = friction. Too loose = fabric moving across skin = friction. The sweet spot: snug enough to stay in place, loose enough to not dig in. This applies to everything: bra, shorts, belt, socks.

Running belts specifically: a belt that's too loose slides and creates friction on your hip bones. A belt with silicone grip stays in place without requiring uncomfortable tightness — eliminating the waistband chafing problem entirely.

When Prevention Fails

If you're mid-run and feel the burn starting:

  1. Don't ignore it. Chafing only gets worse with more friction. It never gets better during a run.
  2. Apply lubricant immediately. If you're carrying Body Glide: apply now. If not: in an emergency, even water can temporarily reduce friction (it evaporates quickly but buys you a mile).
  3. Adjust clothing. If a bra strap is the culprit: shift it slightly. If shorts are riding up: pull them down and tuck.

Post-Chafe Treatment

After the run: gentle soap and water (it will sting — sorry). Pat dry. Apply healing ointment: Aquaphor, A+D ointment, or aloe vera. Avoid tight clothing over the affected area for 24-48 hours. Most chafing heals in 2-3 days.

For severe chafing (broken skin, bleeding): treat like a wound. Clean, antibiotic ointment, bandage. If it doesn't heal within a week or shows signs of infection: see a doctor.

The Long-Term Solution

Once you know YOUR chafing zones (everyone's are slightly different), prevention becomes automatic: Body Glide on those spots before every run, moisture-wicking fabrics only, and gear that fits properly. Chafing is a solved problem — it just requires 30 seconds of prevention before each run.
