The Complete Marathon Training Gear Checklist for Women

You've signed up for your first half or full marathon. The training plan is pinned to your fridge. The running shoes are broken in. But what about everything else? Here's the gear checklist that experienced women marathoners wish they'd had before their first race.

The Non-Negotiables

Running Shoes (Fitted, Not Guessed)

Go to a specialty running store. Get your gait analyzed. Women's feet have different proportions than men's — a women's-specific last (the mold a shoe is built around) makes a meaningful difference. Budget $120-$180 for a quality pair and replace every 300-500 miles.

Sports Bra That Actually Works

At mile 18, you'll thank yourself for investing in a high-support bra designed for running. Encapsulation bras (separate cups) outperform compression bras (smash-and-flatten) for C+ cups. Try: SheFit, Brooks Dare, or Panache Sport. Replace every 6-9 months as elastic degrades.

Anti-Chafe Everything

Marathon-distance chafing hits places you don't expect: sports bra band, inner thighs, underarms, and the waistband of your shorts. Body Glide or Squirrel's Nut Butter applied before every long run. Carry a small stick in your running belt for mid-run reapplication.

Carrying Essentials: The Running Belt Question

For training runs over 8 miles, you need to carry: phone (safety + tracking), fuel (gels or chews every 45 minutes), hydration plan (handheld, vest, or belt-mounted flask), and potentially keys, cash, or ID.

The carrying system matters more than you think. An armband works for phone-only carries but limits capacity. A running vest handles everything but feels heavy for runs under 15 miles. A running belt hits the sweet spot: enough capacity for phone + 4 gels + valuables, light enough to forget it's there.

Key features for marathon training: separate pockets (so your sticky gel wrapper doesn't touch your phone), silicone grip (bounce gets exponentially worse with fatigue), and a pocket large enough for your actual phone with a case (not the phone model from 2019).

Fuel Strategy

Energy Gels

Start practicing with gels on training runs at least 8 weeks before race day. Your gut needs to learn to process sugar while running. Popular options: GU (reliable, wide flavor range), Maurten (gel-like texture, easier on stomach), Spring Energy (real food ingredients). Take one every 45 minutes starting at mile 6.

Hydration

For training runs with available water fountains: carry a handheld or rely on fountains. For runs without water access: a hydration vest or belt-mounted flask. Practice your race-day hydration strategy in training — don't try anything new on race morning.

Race Day Additions

  • Nipple covers — yes, women need them too (sports bra seams + sweat + 26.2 miles = problem)
  • Hair ties + headband — your hair strategy needs to survive 3-5 hours of sweat
  • Timing chip attachment — whatever belt or bib system you're using, practice with it
  • Throwaway layer — an old sweatshirt you can discard at the start line (it's cold at 6 AM)
  • Post-race bag — dry clothes, flip-flops, snacks, phone charger at the finish line

The Gear Trap

Here's what experienced marathoners know: gear matters, but less than you think. The single most important piece of equipment is your training plan consistency. A runner in $40 shoes who trains consistently will beat a runner in $200 shoes who trains sporadically every time. Buy smart, train hard, and trust the process.
