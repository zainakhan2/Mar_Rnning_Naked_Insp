The Complete Guide to parkrun for Beginners

Every Saturday morning, in 23 countries, over 200,000 people line up to run, jog, or walk 5 kilometers — for free. No entry fee. No minimum pace. No judgment. This is parkrun, and it might be the best thing to happen to running since the invention of the sports bra.

What Is parkrun?

parkrun is a free, weekly, timed 5K event that happens every Saturday morning at 9:00 AM in parks around the world. It's not a race — it's a "timed run." You can run, jog, walk, push a stroller, or walk your dog (on a short lead). Everyone is welcome. Every. Single. Person.

There are 2,000+ parkrun locations worldwide. The US has 500+ events and growing. To find your nearest: parkrun.us (US) or parkrun.com (global).

How It Works

  1. Register once — free, forever, at parkrun.com. You get a barcode
  2. Print your barcode — or save a screenshot on your phone
  3. Show up Saturday — arrive by 8:45 AM for first-timer briefing
  4. Run/walk the course — 5K (3.1 miles), usually 2-3 laps of a park
  5. Scan your barcode — at the finish, a volunteer scans you
  6. Get your time — emailed to you + posted on the parkrun website

Why parkrun Is Perfect for Beginners

There's no minimum pace

The last person to finish gets as much applause as the first. Some parkrunners finish in 16 minutes. Some finish in 60 minutes. Both are parkrunners. Both get their barcode scanned. Both get a time on the results page.

It's free

No entry fee. Ever. parkrun is funded by sponsors and staffed entirely by volunteers. Every parkrun event has a "volunteer roster" — you can volunteer instead of running (it counts as participation).

It's social

parkrun is as much a social event as a running event. Most events have a coffee meetup afterward at a nearby cafe. You'll meet the same faces each week. Friendships form. Running buddies emerge. The Saturday morning routine becomes something you look forward to, not something you endure.

What to Bring

  • Your barcode — printed or on phone (screenshot, not just the email)
  • Running shoes — that's it for gear. No special clothing required
  • Something to carry your phone — an armband for 5K, or a running belt if you also carry keys/card
  • Water — optional for 5K. Most parkruns don't have water stations
  • A friend — optional but recommended. parkrun is better shared

parkrun Etiquette

  • Don't wear headphones on both ears — keep one ear free for safety and marshals
  • Say thank you to volunteers — they're giving up their Saturday morning so you can run
  • Move left to let faster runners pass — standard running etiquette
  • Don't start at the front — unless you're running sub-20. Start in the middle or back
  • Clap for the finishers behind you — especially the last few. They need it most

Your First parkrun Plan

If you've never run 5K: walk/run it. Walk 3 minutes, jog 1 minute. Repeat for the full 5K. Your time doesn't matter. Finishing matters. The second time will be faster. The third time will feel natural. By the tenth time: you'll wonder how you ever spent Saturday mornings any other way.
