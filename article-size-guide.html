How to Measure Yourself for a Running Belt (And Why Most Size Charts Are Wrong)

Sizing confusion is the #1 reason for running belt returns. Not defects. Not dissatisfaction. Simply ordering the wrong size because the size chart was confusing, the measurement instructions were vague, or the belt arrived and fit differently than expected.

Why Most Size Charts Fail

Most running belt brands give you a waist measurement and two sizes: S/M or L/XL (or sometimes S, M, L, XL). The problem: "waist" means different things to different people. Your natural waist (narrowest point above your belly button) might be 28 inches. But you don't wear a running belt on your natural waist — you wear it on your hips, which might be 36 inches. Using the wrong measurement = wrong size.

The Correct Measurement Method

Here's how to measure for a running belt in 60 seconds:

  1. Stand naturally — don't suck in, don't push out. Stand how you stand.
  2. Find the position — place two fingers horizontally below your belly button. That's where the belt sits. Not on your waist. Not on your hips. On the high hip — about 2 inches below your navel.
  3. Wrap the tape measure — snug but not tight. You should be able to slide a finger between the tape and your skin.
  4. Read the number — this is your running belt size, in inches.

What If You're Between Sizes?

This is where most people get stuck. If the size chart says S/M fits 25"-33" and L/XL fits 33"-43", and you measure exactly 33"... which one?

Go with the larger size. Here's why:

  • A too-tight belt is uncomfortable and restricts breathing on long runs
  • A too-loose belt can be tightened (if it has an adjustable buckle)
  • Your waist measurement fluctuates 1-2 inches throughout the day (hydration, meals)
  • During marathon training, your body composition changes week to week

An adjustable belt (like StrideHer) makes this even simpler: the buckle adjusts across the full range, so a 33" runner in L/XL simply tightens the buckle to their ideal tension.

The Clothing Size Shortcut

If you don't have a tape measure handy:

  • US women's sizes 0-10: S/M will almost certainly fit
  • US women's sizes 10-20+: L/XL is your size
  • Right at size 10: L/XL for comfort (you can always tighten)

This isn't a perfect correlation (body proportions vary widely), but it's a useful starting point when measurement isn't available.

How the Belt Should Feel

A properly fitted running belt should feel:

  • Snug — it shouldn't slide freely when you tug it
  • Not tight — you should be able to breathe fully without restriction
  • Level — the belt should sit horizontal, not tilted or riding up
  • Secure during movement — jump 10 times. Does it move? If yes: tighten. If it stays put: perfect.

The "jump test" is the best at-home fit check. Put on the belt, load it with your phone and keys, and do 10 jumping jacks. Zero movement = correct fit. Any bouncing or sliding = adjust the buckle or switch sizes.
