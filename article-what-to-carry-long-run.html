What to Carry on a Long Run: The Complete Packing List

There's a tipping point somewhere around mile 6 where running stops being a "just grab your shoes" activity and becomes a logistics operation. Here's exactly what to carry, when to carry it, and how to carry it all without feeling like a pack mule.

The Distance Tiers

Under 5 Miles: Phone + Key

For short runs, you need almost nothing. Phone for safety and music. House key. That's it. An armband or a pocket in your shorts handles this. No belt, no vest, no hydration pack. Keep it simple.

5-10 Miles: Phone + Key + Fuel + Card

This is where a running belt becomes valuable. At 45 minutes or longer, you may want an energy gel. You might be far enough from home that carrying a credit card or cash makes sense (in case you need water, an Uber, or emergency supplies). A single-pocket armband can't hold all this. A 3-pocket belt can.

10-15 Miles: The Full Kit

Half-marathon training territory. You now need 2-3 gels (one every 45 minutes), your phone, ID (always carry ID on runs over 10 miles — if something goes wrong, responders need to know who you are), cash or card, and potentially period products. This is where pocket organization matters — you don't want to dig past your keys and cash to find a gel at mile 11.

15+ Miles: Belt + Hydration

Marathon training runs. Everything from the 10-15 mile list, plus: 4-6 gels, salt/electrolyte tabs, and a hydration plan. Some runners carry a handheld water bottle alongside their belt. Others plan routes past water fountains. For runs over 20 miles, consider a hydration vest that supplements your belt.

The Carry Priority Matrix

Not everything is equally important. Here's the hierarchy:

  1. Phone (safety — always, every run, non-negotiable)
  2. House key (unless someone is home)
  3. Fuel (gels/chews for any run over 45 minutes)
  4. ID (runs over 10 miles or solo runs in remote areas)
  5. Cash/card ($20 emergency backup)
  6. Period products (if applicable — a belt with a hidden pocket solves this discreetly)
  7. Lip balm / body glide (longer runs in sun or cold)
  8. Salt tabs (runs over 90 minutes in heat)

How to Carry It

The mistake most runners make: cramming everything into one pocket or one armband. The result: items tangle, gels become inaccessible, and your phone screen gets scratched by your keys.

The solution: separate compartments. Phone gets its own zippered pocket (secure, accessible, protected). Fuel gets a pocket with quick access (magnetic snap or easy zipper — you need gels without stopping). Everything else goes in a third pocket where it stays organized and out of the way.

This three-pocket system is why running belts with separate compartments outperform tube-style belts and armbands for any run over 5 miles. Your needs grow with your distance — your carry system should grow with them.

The Weight Test

A fully loaded running belt should weigh under 400 grams (phone + 4 gels + keys + card). That's less than a can of soda. If your carry system feels heavy: you're carrying too much, or your belt is distributing weight poorly. The weight should sit on your hips (stable) not your waist (bouncy). A well-designed belt makes 400 grams feel like nothing by mile 3.
